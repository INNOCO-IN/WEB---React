Shadow Shifter

How might we become protagonists of our own lives?

Bring it to your group
What It Is

Two residential days built on four words: light, shadow, shift, capacity.

Nine challenges across two days, all of them hands-on — and some of them deliberately handed to the youngest people in the room to co-facilitate, because facilitation is a capacity too, and it is learned by doing it.

Why

“When an object finds light, it creates a shadow. And we can change the shape of our own shadow through our actions.”

What shifts afterwards is small and specific. One participant's parents noticed he had started listening to them. Another wrote about failure, and about wanting to look at service without fear. Nearly everyone asked about helping to facilitate the next one.

What Happens
Meditative drawing
Noticing the light and shadow qualities in the people around you.
Mindful walk
Morning, outdoors, unhurried — before any talking begins.
Wall murals
Drawn straight onto the wall, at full size, together.
Case clinic
One person's real challenge, listened to by everyone in the circle.
Role model
Built out of LEGO — who you are trying to become, made small and solid.
A letter
Each participant writes to themselves, to be opened later.
Format

Nine challenges, chosen with you.

The frame stays — light, shadow, shift, capacity — while the activities, the length, and how much your own young people co-facilitate are decided together beforehand. The first cohort's verdict was that two days weren't enough; longer versions are possible.
