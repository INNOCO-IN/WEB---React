Lab 1
Two hours, five objects from your own home, and a room that finds out the pull it feels toward something is not private.
What it is
A guided online session for a single group, spread across countries. Each person gathers five objects from their own home and builds a small arrangement: where I stand now, what is pulling me, and what sits in the gap between the two. In triads, someone in another country tells your situation back to you and adds one object to your build. The words for what happened come afterwards, from the room.
When it helps
A cohort or team scattered across time zones and first languages. A group where seniority quietly decides who speaks first. People carrying work they have not yet said out loud. Any circle whose members are each waiting to be told what their part is.
Who it's for
Bridge builders, organisers, students and teams working across languages and borders. Everyone takes part; no experience and no preparation needed.

Inside the session
Eight movements, held to the minute. Every person speaks within the first twelve minutes, in a named order.
12′Check-in — a spoken round: what is outside your window.
4′Stillness — everyone looks away from the screen.
8′Seed — eight unattributed lines; each person picks one.
10′Play — sixty seconds to fetch five objects from home.
30′Making — silent. Here, the pull, and the gap between.
30′Triads — show, two questions, a retelling, one object added.
22′Harvest — private writing, then the room, then a commitment.
6′Check-out — one word each; one question left open.
The turn
A hero's journey has one protagonist. What the room just did has fifteen.
Nobody's build is finished by its builder. Protagonist — protos + agonistes, the first to contend, not the first to win. The group meets the idea after it has produced it.
What groups take away
Each person leaves with one step small enough to take this week, and the name of one person whose story they will support. Both are written down and spoken aloud.
Where it comes from
The session is built on the ME=WE method IN has practised since 2015, with more than 650 people across five countries, documented in a peer-reviewed Springer handbook chapter (2024).
